# FieldBridge Judge Story

## The hook

**A technician can replace the device perfectly and still leave the customer
unable to work.**

A coordinator receives: “Urgent—replace the printer. Send someone today.” It
looks routine. It is not. The fictional contract does not permit the requested
urgency language to set the schedule; its SLA determines the deadline. The site
requires a chaperone, the actual user and required serial evidence are missing,
and the replacement must be registered and reconnected to managed workflows
before anyone schedules a visit.

## Who this is for

FieldBridge is for field-service coordinators and dispatch leads who turn
incomplete tickets into correctly routed, technician-ready work. The recurring
burden is not one lookup; it is reconciling contract SLA, history, site access,
ownership, fulfillment, restoration, and closure evidence before anyone makes a
promise.

## The promise

FieldBridge investigates a thin synthetic ticket in the background and brings
the coordinator one evidence-linked decision card. It interrupts only for a fact
or authority it cannot safely resolve. The deterministic policy is authoritative;
the Strands agent selects evidence and explains the result.

```text
Allowed: inspect → route → recommend → prepare → record review
Blocked: execute dispatch → order → contact → modify production → close
```

## What judges should remember

FieldBridge prepares the contracted-team route, schedule recommendation, point of
contact, and onsite decision packet. A human decides whether a technician is
dispatched. Approval records review of a draft; it never becomes a hidden action
button.

Every organization, ticket, identifier, contract, and result is synthetic. Local
Strands-to-Bedrock execution is verified for both scenarios. AgentCore
deployment, CloudWatch trace, public URL, and measured field impact remain
**UNKNOWN** until independently verified.
